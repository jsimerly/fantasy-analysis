Value starts as points above replacement (PAR): each projected season's points above this league's replacement level (the best player left over after every lineup is filled from the players who actually play that week, so injuries and byes push the line deeper than the Nth starter). PAR / game is the plain version: how many points per game a player gives you over a waiver-wire player at his position, rest of this season. Seasons after this one are discounted at the rate you choose. WAR (wins above replacement) is the same PAR run through this league's own win curve, for comparing across leagues and positions in wins; rank by either. No market input goes into any of it. Fair is what the market would pay if it agreed with our value, two ways. Rank match: line players up by our value and by price; the player we rank 10th gets the 10th-highest price as his fair value. It keeps the market's own price ladder and only argues about who stands where, so inside a flat tier (KTC's 9990s) swaps show as 0% and the Gap column carries the signal. Curve fit: one smooth line through every (our value, price) point, and each player's fair price is read off the line. Mispricing compares the market to that fair value within the player's position, the read to trade on; All positions does it with one line across every position, which also carries the model's positional lean (it gives quarterbacks a larger share of league wins than the market or the realized seasons do, so receivers read rich there), so take that column with the Performance tab.

How the model is validated. The test sets are seasons: for each season from 2015 to 2022 the model is trained only on earlier seasons, projects that season's players, and is scored against the wins above replacement those players actually delivered over the following years, in this league's units. Nothing from the market enters the score. A random split would leak a player's future into his past, so the split is by time. KTC appears only as context: what you would have done without a model, scored the same way on the same players.

How trades are scored. Every completed trade in the league since 2021, both sides, priced in KTC dynasty value with KTC's own trade calculator, so a two-for-one is judged the way the calculator judges it (the best asset counts for more than its share). At N, the trade date, that is the market's verdict at the time: the fair share is how far the calculator leaned your way. The same assets are then re-priced at N+1, N+2, N+3 years and today: what the market later thought of each package, with a pick turning into the rookie it became. A horizon that has not arrived is blank. Wins delivered since (wins above replacement in this league's units, from the week after the trade) are the secondary column. Pick the horizon that drives the scorecards, filter by league, manager and trade age, and click a column header to sort.

Where will each team's next pick land? The market prices a pick by its expected slot, so a 1st from a 2-win team is not a 1st from the league leader. This is the empirical answer from 26,000 league-seasons of Sleeper history: given a team's record and points-for rank after k weeks, how often it finished in the Early, Mid or Late third of the draft order (the draft order is reverse standings). Before week 1 the prior is last season's finish. The Trades tab prices every traded pick this way at the trade date; this tab shows the model itself, every current team's outlook, and how well the table has called our own leagues' draft orders.

Every roster's value, week by week. KTC's own power ranking replayed over the league's history: each week every held player and pick is priced on KTC dynasty (superflex, TE premium once KTC published it), ranked within the roster and depth-weighted the way KTC's team page does it, with the top team that week pinned at 99. So the default line is standing, not raw total: switch to "vs league average" or the plain sum to see level moves the 1–99 scale hides. Grey bands are regular seasons (NFL week 1 to the league's final week); dashed green lines are draft days. Click a team to highlight it; hover the chart for that week's standings.

How the market prices over time. KTC's own daily history since 2020 (superflex dynasty values, players priced at 1,000 or more), measured for its regularities. Every relative move is market-adjusted: a player's change minus the median priced player's change, because KTC rescales its 1–9,999 scale and the whole pool drifts with the calendar. Nothing here is modelled; it is what the pricing community does, and where it is systematically off against what players then delivered.

Value to a specific roster is not the league value: it is the wins a player adds to that lineup (optimal lineup with him minus without him, at that roster's own point on the win curve).
